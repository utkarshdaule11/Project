## 3. Task 2 — Municipal Elevation Provenance Audit

The audit mandated verifying the exact coordinate reference point, dataset source, sampling method, reproducibility, and confidence level for all 14 elevation values, with explicit instruction:
> *"Do not describe a value as a verified municipal centroid elevation unless the municipal boundary and centroid calculation are documented. If the available evidence only supports an approximate city elevation, say so. Do not change any elevation values or strings."*

### 3.1 Methodological Distinction: Centroid vs. City-Center Reference

In rigorous GIS and geodesy:
1. **True Municipal Centroid:** Requires acquiring the official administrative municipal polygon boundary ($\partial \Omega$) from the municipal corporation, computing the geometric surface centroid $(\bar{x}, \bar{y}) = \frac{1}{A} \iint_{\Omega} (x, y) \, dA$, and sampling a continuous Digital Elevation Model (DEM) across that boundary.
2. **Project Elevation Reality:** The 14 values in `content/states.ts` were **not derived from polygon geometric centroid integrations**. Instead, they represent **Approximate City-Center Geodetic Reference Spot Heights** derived from Survey of India toposheet spot heights, central municipal points (e.g. Lal Chowk, Zero Mile Stone, Kartavya Path, BBMP ridge), or central 30m SRTM DEM pixel values.
3. **Documentation Guardrail:** While the string suffix in `content/states.ts` reads `(Municipal Centroid Elevation)` (introduced editorially in Step 2 to signal an urban macro height rather than a highway pavement gradient), **geodetically these values are Approximate City-Center Reference Elevations**.
